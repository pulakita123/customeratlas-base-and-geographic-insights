# Key Findings & Business Interpretation

## Findings recorded in the original project notes
The existing README describes three broad patterns:

1. **Geographic and company-level concentration:** the customer base is not evenly distributed across all countries and companies.
2. **Longer-tenure customers:** a significant portion of the customer base falls into longer-tenure groups, which may indicate a relatively established customer base.
3. **Variation by subscription year:** customer additions differ across subscription years rather than following one uniform pattern.

These are qualitative summaries carried forward from the original project description. They are not independently recalculated in this documentation update.

## What these patterns may mean

### 1. Customer concentration
If a large share of customers comes from a small number of countries or companies, leadership may want to understand its exposure to those segments and whether growth opportunities exist elsewhere.

**Further analysis:** calculate each country's and company's percentage share, compare concentration over time and investigate the business importance of each segment.

### 2. Customer tenure
A sizeable longer-tenure segment can suggest that many customer relationships have existed for an extended period. However, tenure alone does not establish satisfaction, engagement, renewal likelihood or profitability.

**Further analysis:** compare tenure with renewal, usage, satisfaction and revenue data if available.

### 3. Customer additions by year
Differences in customer additions may help identify periods of stronger or weaker growth.

**Further analysis:** compare complete periods, check for partial-year effects and connect the trend to marketing, sales or market-entry information before suggesting a cause.

## Business recommendations
- Monitor customer concentration by country and company rather than relying on total customer count alone.
- Review tenure segments alongside renewal and engagement measures before designing retention actions.
- Investigate changes in customer additions with supporting sales or marketing evidence.
- Keep KPI definitions and the reporting reference date consistent across refreshes.
